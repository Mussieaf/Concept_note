Message from the Host

His Excellency Mr. Takele Mamo

The African Union's Agenda 2063 gives paramount attention to gender equality and environmental sustainability as pillars for continental transformation.

Based on this strategic vision, the African First Ladies Initiative (AFLI) has endorsed the priority of emission reduction and poverty alleviation since 2025, improving women's access to green technology, clean energy, and sustainable livelihoods through intensive training, capacity building, policy advocacy, and international forums.

Thus, the Initiative has scheduled a High-Level Forum to be undertaken on March 17–19, 2026, at the premises of the National Palace in Addis Ababa. The event provides a unique networking opportunity to link First Ladies, government officials, international organizations, and private sector partners including Banks, TOYO Solar, Globo Hydro Power, and Ethiopian Conformity Assessement Enterprises.

This engagement will lead to shared experiences and feedback that will empower the next generation of women leaders ready to combat climate change with ethical values and innovative solutions.

Therefore, the Office of the Host of the African First Ladies Initiative would like to extend our gratitude to all partners including SEI, AIPRC, and Union Global Investment for facilitating this forum in creating opportunities for women across the continent. Welcome to Addis Ababa. Welcome to the future of African climate leadership.

His Excellency Mr. Takele Mamo

CEO, Acacia Trading PLC & Host of AFLI Forum 2026

The forum's unique approach recognizes the inextricable link between environmental sustainability and economic development. By focusing on women-led solutions, we aim to unlock the untapped potential of African women as drivers of climate innovation.

Keynote Address

Transformative Leadership in the Climate Era

[Keynote Speaker]

Dr. [Name]

Climate Economist & Advisor

Previous: UN Climate Panel

Focus: Gender-responsive climate finance

Your Excellencies, distinguished delegates, and champions of African development. We stand at a critical juncture where the decisions we make today will determine the prosperity of our continent for generations to come.

The statistics are sobering yet motivating: Africa contributes less than 4% of global emissions yet bears the disproportionate burden of climate impacts. Meanwhile, women represent 50% of our agricultural workforce but receive less than 10% of climate adaptation funding.

"The green transition in Africa will be led by women, or it will not succeed. This is not sentiment—it is economics." — Dr. [Name], Keynote Speaker

Consider the transformative potential of clean cooking solutions alone. Over 900 million Africans rely on solid biomass for cooking, resulting in 600,000 premature deaths annually—predominantly women and children. The transition to clean cooking represents a $20 billion market opportunity that simultaneously addresses health, climate, and gender equality goals.

But technology alone is insufficient. We need policy frameworks that recognize women's land rights, financial instruments that de-risk green investments, and leadership that champions inclusive decision-making.

This forum must be the turning point where commitment translates to action, where pledges become projects, and where African women take their rightful place at the center of the climate solution.

Fireside Chat

Clean Cooking Innovation

Transforming Household Energy for Health, Climate, and Prosperity

The clean cooking transition represents one of Africa's most significant opportunities for simultaneous climate mitigation, health improvement, and women's economic empowerment. This intimate fireside chat brings together pioneers who are scaling access to modern energy cooking services across the continent.

Traditional cooking methods using wood, charcoal, and dung account for approximately 2% of global greenhouse gas emissions—equivalent to the aviation industry. The health impacts are equally staggering, with indoor air pollution causing more deaths than malaria, HIV/AIDS, and tuberculosis combined.

Innovation Spotlight

The chat will feature demonstrations of next-generation electric pressure cookers, ethanol stoves, and biogas systems designed specifically for African cooking practices. These technologies reduce cooking time by 50% and fuel costs by 70%, freeing women for education and income-generating activities.

"A woman who cooks with clean energy gains four hours per week—time she can invest in her children, her business, or her community."

Panel Session II

Green Livelihoods & Poverty Alleviation

March 18, 09:00–11:00 | National Palace Conference Hall

[Panel Session]

Climate action and poverty reduction are mutually reinforcing. This session examines how green economy transitions can create dignified employment for millions of African women currently trapped in subsistence agriculture and informal sector work.

From solar entrepreneurship to sustainable forestry, green sectors offer pathways out of poverty that build rather than deplete natural capital. Panelists will share concrete strategies for skills development, market access, and financial inclusion.

The session will culminate in the launch of the AFLI Green Livelihoods Fund, a $100 million initiative to support women-led climate enterprises across 20 African countries.

Featured Interventions

Great Green Wall

Women's participation in Africa's flagship reforestation initiative has created 1.2 million jobs while restoring 18 million hectares of degraded land. The panel discusses scaling this model.

Blue Economy

Sustainable aquaculture and coastal restoration initiatives led by women in Senegal, Madagascar, and Kenya demonstrate the potential of ocean-based climate solutions.

Addis Ababa Declaration

Forum Declaration

March 19, 2026 | Unanimously Adopted by Delegates

We, the African First Ladies, gathered at the National Palace in Addis Ababa from 17–19 March 2026, recognizing the existential threat of climate change to our continent and the transformative potential of women's leadership in addressing this crisis, do hereby declare:

Article 1: We commit to championing gender-responsive climate policies in our respective nations, ensuring that National Determined Contributions include specific targets for women's participation in climate decision-making at all levels.

Article 2: We pledge to mobilize resources for women-led climate enterprises, working with financial institutions to de-risk green investments and increase access to climate finance for women entrepreneurs.

Article 3: We resolve to prioritize universal access to clean cooking energy by 2030, recognizing this as both a climate imperative and a fundamental health and gender equality issue.

Article 4: We commit to establishing national AFLI chapters that will sustain the momentum of this forum, tracking progress and holding stakeholders accountable to commitments made.

Article 5: We call upon the international community to honor climate finance commitments, with at least 30% of funding directed to gender-responsive projects designed and implemented by African women.

Article 6: We pledge to amplify the voices of grassroots women—farmers, entrepreneurs, and community leaders—in national and international climate dialogues.

"The future of Africa is green, and that future will be built by women."

Signed this 19th day of March, 2026

Her Excellency [Name] & Delegates
