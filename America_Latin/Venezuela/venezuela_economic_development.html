Executive Investment Thesis

Click to explore Venezuela's extraordinary resource endowment and industrial vision

▼

On behalf of MAEC CAPITAL LIMITED, we respectfully extend our highest consideration to the Government and people of the Bolivarian Republic of Venezuela.

Following a preliminary review of Venezuela's extraordinary natural-resource endowment, industrial infrastructure, energy position, mineral potential, geographic location and development requirements, we respectfully submit for Your Excellency's consideration a potential Venezuelan Comprehensive Economic Development Funding Program of up to €300 billion, to be deployed progressively over approximately 15 years and subject to independent technical, commercial, legal, environmental and financial due diligence.

"Venezuela should not merely extract and export natural resources. Venezuela should convert its hydrocarbons, natural gas, minerals, agricultural resources and human capital into increasingly higher-value industrial products, infrastructure, exports, employment and sustainable fiscal revenues."

The proposed program is therefore conceived not as a conventional sovereign borrowing program, but as a national industrial transformation platform, in which commercially viable projects would be financed through ring-fenced project companies, state-owned enterprises, private-sector joint ventures, strategic investors, export-credit agencies, development-finance institutions and other appropriately regulated financing channels.

The attached international precedent that informed the architecture of this proposal emphasized pilot projects, non-sovereign borrowing, parastatal/JV companies, natural-resource monetization, infrastructure, industrialization and direct engagement with the Presidency. We propose adapting that architecture to Venezuela while introducing substantially stronger lender protections, independent verification and project-finance discipline.

Five Core Investment Principles

1
Rehabilitate existing productive assets before duplicating them.
2
Monetize natural gas rather than treating it principally as an oil-production by-product.
3
Move from crude exports toward refining, petrochemicals, polymers and industrial chemicals.
4
Move from mineral extraction toward beneficiation, metallurgy, semi-finished products and selected downstream manufacturing.
5
Ensure that major borrowing is supported by project cash flows, export contracts, receivables, offtake agreements, asset security and ring-fenced revenues rather than relying principally on an unconditional sovereign repayment promise.

Venezuela's Extraordinary Asset Base

Venezuela possesses an unusual combination of assets:

1
One of the world's largest proven crude-oil reserves;
2
Very substantial natural-gas reserves;
3
Large iron-ore and bauxite resources;
4
Significant gold potential;
5
Nickel, phosphate, diamonds and reported coltan / critical-mineral occurrences;
6
Existing oil, petrochemical, mining, power, port and pipeline infrastructure that can be rehabilitated and expanded;
7
A Caribbean location strategically positioned between North American, Latin American, European and Atlantic markets;
8
Significant hydroelectric resources;
9
Agricultural land and freshwater resources; and
10
A substantial industrial and engineering knowledge base that can be rebuilt and modernized.

The scale of the resource base is not itself sufficient to justify €300 billion of financing. The financing case must instead rest on the ability to convert those assets into bankable cash-flow-generating industrial platforms.

Resource Opportunity

🛢️ Crude Oil — 303.2 Billion Barrels Proven

The world's largest proven crude-oil reserves, primarily extra-heavy crude of the Orinoco Belt. However, the existence of 303 billion barrels must not be interpreted as 303 billion barrels of immediately economic production.

The principal investment challenge is therefore not geological scarcity but: drilling and well rehabilitation; steam and enhanced-oil-recovery systems; diluent supply; gathering systems; pipelines; upgrading; water treatment; power; storage; ports; refinery conversion; environmental remediation; skilled personnel; and access to international markets.

EIA has previously identified inadequate investment, PDVSA financial constraints, technical limitations and sanctions as major constraints on Venezuela's petroleum development.

Current production remains far below Venezuela's geological potential. EIA data indicate average crude production of approximately 968,000 barrels per day in 2025, while OPEC-reported production was around 1.1 million barrels per day during parts of 2026.

The program should therefore target sustainable production growth, rather than assuming an immediate return to historic production levels.

Indicative Planning Scenario

A preliminary investment-planning scenario could examine:

1
Phase I: approximately 1.2–1.5 million barrels/day;
2
Phase II: approximately 1.5–1.8 million barrels/day;
3
Long-term scenario: approximately 2.0 million barrels/day or higher,

subject to reservoir engineering, drilling results, infrastructure availability, OPEC considerations, market conditions and environmental constraints. These are planning scenarios, not guaranteed production forecasts.

🔥 Natural Gas — 195 Trillion Cubic Feet

Venezuela has approximately 195 trillion cubic feet of natural-gas reserves, representing roughly 73% of South America's reported gas reserves in EIA's assessment. Approximately 80% of produced gas has historically been associated with oil production.

This creates an exceptional industrial opportunity. Instead of viewing gas principally as Gas → reinjection / fuel, the proposed strategy is:

Gas → processing → methane → ammonia → urea → methanol → chemicals → polymers → export products

and:

Gas → NGLs → ethane / propane / butane → olefins → polymers

Offshore gas development also deserves priority. In August 2026, BP, XRG and UCC obtained a license relating to the Loran offshore gas field, demonstrating renewed international interest in Venezuelan gas development.

⛏️ Iron Ore — 14.68 Billion Metric Tonnes

Venezuelan government-linked mineral data have reported approximately 14.68 billion metric tonnes of iron-ore resources, with approximately 3.6 billion tonnes described as proven in the cited 2018 material. However, the terminology in Venezuelan mineral reporting has historically mixed "resources" and "reserves", and therefore these numbers should be independently re-certified before being used as collateral or financing support.

The opportunity is therefore:

Iron ore → beneficiation → pellets → direct-reduced iron → steel → flat products → long products → fabricated steel

rather than simply exporting ore.

🏭 Bauxite and Aluminum — 321.5 Million Tonnes

Government mineral data have cited bauxite resources in the order of hundreds of millions of tonnes, with one cited Venezuelan catalog reporting approximately 321.5 million tonnes and another categorization reporting approximately 99.4 million tonnes as measured material. These figures should be treated as resource estimates requiring independent geological verification, rather than as bankable proven reserves.

Venezuela's existing aluminium industry provides a platform for rehabilitation and expansion. The strategic chain is:

Bauxite → alumina → primary aluminium → billets/slabs → extrusion → automotive/aerospace/construction products → export manufacturing.

🥇 Gold — ~644 Metric Tonnes Resource

Venezuelan mineral documentation has cited a gold resource estimate of approximately 644 metric tonnes, but this is not equivalent to a modern internationally compliant proved-and-probable reserve statement. Independent geological verification, resource classification, environmental assessment and mine planning are therefore essential.

Gold should be developed through transparent, traceable and internationally compliant mining structures, with: chain-of-custody controls; responsible sourcing; anti-money-laundering procedures; environmental monitoring; formalization of artisanal mining; land rehabilitation; and internationally recognized reporting standards.

🌱 Phosphate — ~2.65 Billion Tonnes

Venezuela has historically reported significant phosphate resources. FAO material identifies Venezuela as possessing approximately 2.65 billion tonnes of phosphate-rock reserves, with typical P₂O₅ content of approximately 20–27%, although this is historical data and should not be treated as a current bankable reserve statement without re-verification.

Riecito phosphate has previously been evaluated for partially acidulated phosphate fertilizer production, and FAO documented a potential 150,000-tonne/year PAPR project concept. This provides a foundation for a broader phosphate strategy:

Phosphate rock → phosphoric acid → MAP/DAP → NPK → specialty fertilizers.

💎 Diamonds, Nickel and Critical Minerals

Venezuelan sources have reported substantial occurrences of diamonds, nickel, copper, coltan, niobium/tantalum, molybdenum and other minerals. However, many published figures are historical or classified inconsistently.

USGS production data demonstrate that Venezuela's recent actual mineral output has been far below the theoretical scale of its geological endowment. For example, USGS estimated 2022 iron-ore production at approximately 2.25 million tonnes, bauxite at approximately 250,000 tonnes and primary aluminium at approximately 47,000 tonnes. This gap between resource potential and actual production is itself an investment opportunity.

Critical minerals should therefore enter the program only after: modern geological surveys; airborne geophysics; drilling; JORC/SAMREC/NI 43-101 or equivalent independent reporting; metallurgical testing; environmental and social assessment; and bankable feasibility studies.

Existing Refining Base

Venezuela possesses a historically large refining system. EIA identifies major facilities including the Paraguana Refining Center, Puerto la Cruz and El Palito.

In February 2026, Reuters reported that Venezuela's refineries were operating at approximately 35% of their combined 1.29 million barrels/day nameplate capacity, processing around 450,000 barrels/day.

Therefore, the first refining investment should be rehabilitation and debottlenecking, not immediate abandonment of existing assets.

300-400K

barrels per day screening capacity

A second approximately 300,000–400,000 barrels/day refinery could be evaluated after successful rehabilitation of the existing system and demonstration of sufficient crude availability.

Refinery-to-Petrochemical Integration

The objective should not be to maximize transportation-fuel production alone. The refinery should become the first stage of a broader carbon-and-hydrocarbon value chain:

Crude → Refinery → Naphtha / LPG / Ethane / Propane / Aromatics / Olefins → Ethylene / Propylene / Benzene / Toluene / Xylene → Polyethylene / Polypropylene / PET / Styrenics / Solvents / Synthetic materials → Industrial and consumer manufacturing

This creates substantially more domestic value than crude export.

Fertilizer Strategy

Click to explore urea cost economics and production targets

▼

Can Venezuela Produce Urea at $180/tonne?

This question requires precision.

Potentially as a production-cost target under favorable gas and plant-efficiency assumptions; not as a guaranteed international export selling price.

Urea economics are dominated by natural-gas costs.

Yara's technical framework uses approximately 36 MMBtu of natural gas per tonne of ammonia and approximately 0.58 tonnes of ammonia per tonne of urea, plus approximately 5.2 MMBtu/tonne of process gas for urea production.

Yara's illustrative economics show that at a gas price of approximately $7/MMBtu, gas-related costs alone can be a very large component of total urea cash cost.

Current international prices are materially above $180/t. For example, Fertiglobe reported a Middle East granular urea benchmark of approximately $400/t in Q1 2025, while current 2026 market intelligence places Middle East granular urea around $268/t in Q2 2026 and estimates much higher delivered prices in some destination markets.

The World Bank expects urea prices to remain unusually elevated in 2026 because of supply disruptions and tight markets.

Urea Concept Interpretation

Urea ConceptAppropriate Interpretation
€/$180/t production costAggressive but potentially testable in a very low-cost gas environment
€/$180/t export pricePossible only under specific market/offtake circumstances; not a base-case global price
€/$180/t domestic policy pricePossible with subsidy or strategic agricultural policy
€/$180/t FOB export priceWould be exceptionally competitive and requires very low production/logistics costs
€/$180/t delivered international priceMuch more difficult because freight, insurance, port and financing costs must be added

The appropriate project objective should therefore be:

Design Venezuelan ammonia/urea complexes capable of targeting a long-run cash-cost position around €/$150–220 per metric tonne under low-cost domestic-gas scenarios, while underwriting the project against a wider urea selling-price range.

This is substantially more defensible than assuming that Venezuela can permanently sell urea internationally at $180/t.

Venezuela already has demonstrated fertilizer infrastructure. Pequiven reported historical operation of an ammonia plant at approximately 1,500 tonnes/day and a urea plant at approximately 2,200 tonnes/day at the José Antonio Anzoátegui complex.

The program should therefore rehabilitate existing plants and subsequently evaluate:

1
1.0–1.5 million tonnes/year ammonia trains;
2
1.5–2.0 million tonnes/year urea trains;
3
DAP/MAP/NPK complexes;
4
nitric acid;
5
ammonium nitrate;
6
calcium ammonium nitrate;
7
specialty fertilizers.

These capacities are screening concepts, not committed production forecasts.

Non-Sovereign Financing Principle

Consistent with the structural concept in the earlier international proposal, the preferred model is:

Government → policy / concessions / regulatory framework
National Development Holding / Investment Authority → equity / strategic oversight
Project SPV / JV → project borrowing
Project cash flows → debt service
Export revenues → repayment and government revenues.

The objective is to minimize unnecessary sovereign balance-sheet exposure.

However, unlike an unconditional "no government liability" proposition, every financing instrument must be examined under Venezuelan law, applicable international law, creditor requirements and the specific security package.

No SBLC, insurance policy, guarantee or indemnity should be represented as eliminating economic risk.

The U.S. Treasury's OFAC Venezuela program currently contains multiple general licenses affecting oil, gas, minerals, investment and related activities.

Every transaction must therefore undergo:

3

4

Economic Context

The World Bank reports that Venezuela's GDP was approximately €/$100 billion-equivalent in 2025 in current U.S.-dollar terms, with real GDP growth of approximately 1.6%. The IMF's April 2026 database projects nominal GDP of approximately $111.3 billion in 2026, while simultaneously reporting extremely high inflation.

Against this economic base, €300 billion is enormous. It cannot responsibly be deployed rapidly. It should therefore be treated as a multi-cycle capital platform, with each subsequent phase dependent upon demonstrated performance of the preceding phase.

Self-Check / Investment Discipline Review

Click to view the investment discipline framework

▼

Before submission to any sovereign, lender or institutional investor, the following points should be retained:

1
No €300 billion unconditional financing commitment has been asserted. The figure is presented as a proposed program envelope.
2
No geological resource has been treated automatically as a bankable reserve. This is particularly important for gold, iron, bauxite, diamonds, coltan and phosphate.
3
No $180/t urea export-price guarantee has been made. The distinction between production cost, FOB price, domestic price and delivered international price is explicit.
4
No refinery return has been guaranteed. The two refinery concepts require crude assays, product-market studies, FEED, EPC pricing and FID.
5
No guaranteed repayment period has been promised. Project cash flows must be demonstrated before debt is incurred.
6
Sanctions have been treated as a live financing constraint. OFAC currently maintains multiple Venezuela-related licenses and restrictions, making transaction-specific legal review mandatory.
7
Existing infrastructure has been prioritized before greenfield duplication. This materially improves capital efficiency.
8
