About The Project

Modern Dairy,
Ethiopian Roots

Established by Milkomi Trading Plc. in Addis Ababa City Administration, our project represents a bold step toward transforming Ethiopia's dairy sub-sector. With over 15 years of agricultural experience, we are introducing intensive, market-oriented dairy farming on a 6-hectare sustainable campus.

Ethiopia holds the largest livestock inventory in Africa, yet commercialization remains low. Milkomi is here to change that narrative — combining exotic breed management with local expertise to deliver premium milk, cheese, butter, and yoghurt to households and businesses across the region.

Green Mission

Sustainable by Design

Our farm integrates climate-smart agriculture with economic growth. We believe Ethiopia's dairy future must be green, inclusive, and resilient.

  • 🌿

    Fodder Self-Sufficiency

    On-site alfalfa, maize silage, and pasture production reduce supply chain emissions and ensure year-round nutrition for our herd.

  • ♻️

    Zero-Waste Processing

    Every by-product is repurposed — from compost sales (ETB 5,000/month) to recycled water systems in our milk processing plant.

  • 👨‍👩‍👧

    Community Employment

    100 direct jobs for skilled and unskilled workers, with priority given to residents of surrounding Addis Ababa communities.

  • 🐄

    Ethical Herd Management

    Veterinary clinics on-site, humane housing, and genetic improvement programs for healthier, higher-yielding cattle.

Strategic Analysis

SWOT Assessment

A comprehensive look at our competitive position in Ethiopia's emerging formal dairy market.

Strengths

  • New modern facility preferred over elderly competitors
  • Closeness to highway increases logistics efficiency
  • 15+ years of agricultural experience by promoter
  • Proven track record of raising quality herd replacements
  • Strong family commitment to farming excellence

Weaknesses

  • Concept is not new; must compete for market share
  • Land size may be a constraint if demand exceeds capacity
  • Need to improve herd production record systems
  • Lack of experience with formal financial records
  • High initial capital requirements

Opportunities

  • Government affirmative actions on modern dairy industry
  • Investment loan opportunities from multiple banks
  • Growing urban demand due to rising disposable incomes
  • Strong local dairy community with business support
  • Currently high fluid milk prices favor capital investment

Threats

  • High cost of capital in Ethiopia
  • Historical volatility of milk market prices
  • Farm operating costs rising faster than milk prices
  • Difficulty finding and retaining dependable farm labor
  • Continually increasing property taxes
