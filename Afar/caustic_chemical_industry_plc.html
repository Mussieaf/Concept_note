2. Business Overview

Caustic Chemical Industry PLC proposes to develop Ethiopia's first large-scale, fully integrated chlor-alkali plant to meet domestic demand and substitute imports of caustic soda, hydrochloric acid, and related chemicals. The facility will utilize Lake Afdera's abundant solar-evaporated salt reserves (290 million tons) as a raw material.

Located 200 km from Djibouti Port, the project benefits from a strategic logistical corridor for both import of machinery and export of chemical products to regional markets including Kenya, Sudan, and Somalia.

2.1. Overview of the Project

Caustic Chemical Industry PLC is an industrial enterprise established to develop and operate Ethiopia's first large-scale chlor-alkali manufacturing plant. The company aims to bridge Ethiopia's critical dependency on imported basic chemicals—particularly caustic soda (NaOH)—which are essential inputs for the textile, paper, soap, and water treatment industries. The proposed facility, to be located on a 500,000 m² industrial plot in Semera, Afar Region, will produce 400 tons per day (TPD) of caustic soda (equivalent to 120,000 metric tons per year) along with key by-products: chlorine (Cl₂), hydrogen (H₂), hydrochloric acid (HCl), and sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl).

This project leverages Ethiopia's abundant solar-evaporated salt reserves at Lake Afdera, estimated at over 290 million tons, as a sustainable and cost-effective raw material source. Its strategic location, only 200 km from the Port of Djibouti, enables easy access to export markets in Kenya, Sudan, Djibouti, and other East African nations. The establishment of the plant supports Ethiopia's Growth and Transformation Plan (GTP II) by promoting import substitution, foreign exchange savings, industrial diversification, and job creation.

2.2. Project Goals and Mission

Vision: To become a regional leader in chlor-alkali chemical production, driving sustainable industrialization and advancing clean manufacturing technologies across East Africa.

Mission: To establish Ethiopia's first environmentally responsible caustic soda manufacturing facility that meets domestic demand, fosters industrial self-reliance, and adheres to international environmental and safety standards.

  • Export Promotion: Supply surplus production to regional markets such as Kenya, Sudan, and Djibouti.

  • Economic Diversification: Strengthen Ethiopia's chemical manufacturing base and contribute to national GDP under the Industrial Parks and Green Growth Strategy.

5. Manufacturing and Operations

5.1. Plant Design and Technology

5.1.1. Process Overview

The chlor-alkali process is based on the electrolysis of brine (sodium chloride solution) to produce caustic soda (NaOH), chlorine gas (Cl₂), and hydrogen gas (H₂) according to the reaction:

2NaCl + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + Cl₂ + H₂

This process forms the core of the chemical industry and is essential for producing a variety of downstream chemicals, including hydrochloric acid (HCl) and sodium hypochlorite (NaOCl).

Primary Raw Materials:

  • Industrial Salt (NaCl) – sourced locally (Lake Afdera)
  • Water – for brine preparation and cooling
  • Electricity – high-voltage DC for electrolysis

Main Products & Co-products:

Chlorine (Cl₂)Gas / LiquefiedHCl, NaOCl, PVC, bleaching agents
Hydrogen (H₂)GasFuel, HCl production, sale as by-product
Hydrochloric Acid (HCl)LiquidIndustrial chemical, water treatment
Sodium Hypochlorite (NaOCl)SolutionDisinfectant, water treatment

Simplified Process Steps

1. Brine Preparation & Purification

  • Dissolve high-purity salt in water → brine
  • Remove impurities (Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺) using lime and soda ash
  • Filter & concentrate brine (~300 g/L NaCl)

2. Electrolysis (Membrane Cell Technology)

  • Brine enters electrolyzer
  • Anode Reaction: 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻
  • Cathode Reaction: 2H₂O + 2e⁻ → H₂ + 2OH⁻
  • Ion-exchange membrane separates products, producing high-purity NaOH

3. Product Concentration & Handling

  • NaOH concentrated to 50% solution or flakes
  • Cl₂ dried, liquefied, or converted to HCl/NaOCl
  • H₂ cooled, compressed, and stored or used for HCl synthesis

5.1.2. Technology Selection: Ion-Exchange Membrane (IEM) Process

The project employs Ion-Exchange Membrane (IEM) electrolysis, the most advanced and energy-efficient chlor-alkali technology available. This process uses ion-selective membranes to separate chlorine, hydrogen, and caustic soda, ensuring high purity and eliminating the use of mercury or asbestos (common in older diaphragm and mercury cell technologies).

Key Advantages of IEM Technology:

  • Produces high-purity NaOH (≥50%) suitable for industrial applications.
  • Reduces chlorine contamination in caustic soda.
  • Requires lower energy consumption (3,200–3,500 kWh/MT NaOH).
  • Environmentally friendly, with zero mercury or asbestos discharge.
  • Enables by-product recovery of H₂ and Cl₂ for secondary product manufacturing (HCl, NaOCl).

The process is controlled by a Distributed Control System (DCS) that automates brine feed, electrolysis, product recovery, and safety monitoring.

5.4. Production Process and Capacity

5.4.1. Step-by-Step Process Description

1. Brine Preparation and Purification

  • Salt Dissolution: Raw NaCl is dissolved in process water to form brine (~300 g/L concentration).
  • Chemical Purification: Brine is treated with soda ash (Na₂CO₃) and lime (CaO) to remove Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ impurities.
  • Filtration and Ion Exchange: The brine passes through dual media filters and ion-exchange polishers for final purification before electrolysis.
  • Storage: Purified brine is stored in large SS tanks for continuous feed to electrolyzers.

2. Electrolysis (Membrane Cell)

  • Anode Reaction: 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂(gas) + 2e⁻
  • Cathode Reaction: 2H₂O + 2e⁻ → H₂(gas) + 2OH⁻
  • Product Streams: Chlorine gas is released at the anode, hydrogen and sodium hydroxide form at the cathode.
  • NaOH Collection: Caustic soda solution (~30–32%) is collected and transferred to evaporators.

3. Product Concentration and Conversion

  • NaOH Concentration: Evaporators raise caustic soda concentration to 48–50% (lye) or dry it into flakes (98%).
  • HCl Production: Hydrogen and chlorine react under controlled combustion to form hydrochloric acid.
  • NaOCl Production: Chlorine reacts with NaOH to produce sodium hypochlorite solution for water treatment use.

4. Product Storage and Packaging

  • NaOH: Stored in 200 m³ rubber-lined steel tanks; packaged in drums or bulk tankers.
  • HCl: Stored in acid-resistant tanks for bulk or drum delivery.
  • NaOCl: Distributed in solution form to water utilities and industries.
  • Cl₂ and H₂: Stored in pressurized steel cylinders or pipelines for sale or internal use.

5.7. Safety Protocols and Environmental Management

5.7.1. Process Safety

The chlor-alkali process involves handling hazardous gases (chlorine and hydrogen), requiring stringent safety and environmental measures:

  • Gas Leak Detection: Continuous monitoring systems for Cl₂ and H₂.
  • Emergency Scrubbers: Neutralize chlorine leaks using caustic soda solution.
  • Hydrogen Safety: Flame arrestors, vent stacks, and explosion-proof electricals.
  • Personal Protective Equipment (PPE): Mandatory use of respirators, gloves, goggles, and acid-resistant suits.
  • Emergency Response Systems: Onsite fire suppression, chlorine neutralization pit, and trained emergency response teams.

5.7.2. Environmental Controls

  • Zero-Mercury and Asbestos-Free Technology (membrane process).
  • Effluent Treatment Plant (ETP) for neutralizing acidic or saline discharges (maintaining pH 6–9, TSS <50 mg/L per EPA guidelines).
  • Closed-Loop Cooling System to minimize water consumption.
  • Air Pollution Control: Chlorine absorbers and gas scrubbers maintain emissions within Proclamation No. 300/2002 limits.
  • Waste Management: Spent brine recycled; solid residues sent to authorized disposal facilities.

The plant's Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) and Occupational Safety Management Plan comply with Ethiopian standards under Proclamation Nos. 299/2013, 300/2002, and 1156/2019.

6.5. Plant Layout Principles

The plant layout is based on the following core design principles:

Linear Process Flow: The layout ensures smooth material movement from raw salt intake to final product dispatch. Major process steps include: Brine preparation → electrolysis → caustic soda concentration → by-product recovery → storage and dispatch.

Functional Zoning: High-risk areas (chlorine and hydrogen handling) are located at the farthest end of the site from administrative and public zones. Clean and controlled operations are segregated from storage and logistics areas.

Accessibility and Maintenance: All equipment and processing units are accessible via dedicated maintenance corridors to facilitate inspection, repair, and emergency response.

Future Scalability: Space has been allocated for future production units or renewable energy integration (solar/hydrogen) to enhance plant capacity and sustainability.

6.6. Safety and Environmental Compliance

The plant layout has been developed in full compliance with national and international safety standards, including the IFC Environmental, Health and Safety (EHS) Guidelines and UN Chlorine Institute standards.

Key design features include:

  • Minimum 50–100 m buffer between hazardous chemical storage and administrative zones.
  • Fire lanes and emergency exits provided throughout the plant for rapid evacuation.
  • Chlorine and hydrogen storage areas are isolated, well-ventilated, and equipped with gas leak detection and neutralization systems.
  • Containment bunds under NaOH and HCl tanks to prevent accidental spillage into the environment.
  • Neutralization pits and effluent treatment systems for safe wastewater management.
  • Green buffer zones around the plant to absorb noise and fugitive emissions.

6.7. Utilities and Services Integration

To ensure reliable and continuous operations, utilities are designed with redundancy and efficiency in mind:

  • The power substation (132/40,000 kV) is centrally located for optimal load distribution.
  • Cooling towers and effluent treatment plants are positioned downwind from administrative and clean zones.
  • Dedicated utility corridors for electricity, water, steam, and compressed air minimize interference with production areas.
  • Demineralized water, steam, and instrument air systems are integrated into the automated control network.

6.8. Logistics and Transport

Efficient logistics design supports both raw material inflow and finished product outflow:

  • Separate inbound and outbound routes prevent traffic congestion and contamination risks.
  • Truck loading/unloading bays are positioned adjacent to storage tanks and warehouses for rapid turnaround.
  • Weighbridges, security gates, and firefighting systems are located at key entry points.
  • The internal road network ensures smooth mobility for material handling, maintenance, and emergency response.

  • NaOH tanks are built on reinforced concrete foundations with containment bunds.

9. Environmental and Regulatory Compliance

9.1. Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)

9.1.1. Introduction

This Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) section identifies and evaluates the potential environmental and social impacts associated with the proposed chlor-alkali production plant located in the Afar Region. The EIA aims to ensure that all stages of project planning, design, construction, and operation are conducted in accordance with the Environmental Policy of Ethiopia, the EIA Proclamation No. 299/2002, and relevant international environmental standards.

The assessment highlights possible adverse environmental impacts, proposes mitigation and monitoring measures, and demonstrates the project's commitment to environmental protection, occupational safety, and sustainable resource use.

9.1.2. Potential Environmental Impacts

A. Air Emissions

The primary sources of air emissions will include chlorine gas (Cl₂) leaks, hydrogen (H₂) venting and combustion, and particulate matter generated from salt handling and NaOH flaking operations. While emissions are expected to remain within permissible levels, accidental releases could impact air quality and occupational safety if not properly managed.

  • Possible chlorine gas (Cl₂) leaks during production, transfer, or storage operations.
  • Hydrogen (H₂) combustion risks arising from accidental releases.
  • Particulate emissions generated from salt handling and sodium hydroxide (NaOH) flaking operations.

B. Wastewater

Wastewater will originate mainly from brine purification, cooling tower blowdown, and cleaning activities. The effluent will contain brine sludge (estimated at 405 MT/year) with calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) and magnesium hydroxide (Mg(OH)₂), as well as acidic or alkaline neutralization discharges.

  • Generation of approximately 405 metric tons of brine sludge per year, primarily composed of calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) and magnesium hydroxide (Mg(OH)₂).
  • Discharge of acidic or alkaline effluents resulting from neutralization and cleaning activities.

C. Solid Waste

Solid waste will include spent ion-exchange resins (300 liters/year) and plastic packaging waste (PP/HDPE). Packaging waste is recyclable, while spent resins are categorized as hazardous under Regulation No. 299/2013 and must be handled by licensed contractors.

  • Disposal of spent ion-exchange resins estimated at 300 liters per year.
  • Non-hazardous packaging waste including polypropylene (PP) and high-density polyethylene (HDPE).

D. Noise

Operational equipment such as compressors and electrolyzers will generate noise levels up to 95 dB. Prolonged exposure can impact workers' hearing and create discomfort in nearby communities.

  • High noise levels, reaching up to 95 dB, generated by compressors, pumps, and electrolyzers during operation.

9.2. Mitigation Measures

9.2.1. Air Quality Control

  • Install wet scrubbers and flare stacks for chlorine and hydrogen control.
  • Deploy chlorine leak detectors, automatic shut-off valves, and explosion-proof ventilation.
  • Use bag filters for particulate control in the NaOH flaking unit.
  • Conduct continuous emission monitoring (CEMS) and emergency response drills.

Regular air quality reports will be submitted to the Ethiopian Environmental Protection Authority (EPA) to confirm compliance.

9.2.2. Wastewater Management

  • Construct sedimentation ponds for sludge settlement; reuse dried sludge in construction applications.
  • Treat effluent via pH-controlled neutralization tanks prior to discharge.
  • Implement a closed-loop water recycling system to reuse up to 80% of treated water.

These measures will significantly reduce freshwater abstraction and ensure protection of local water bodies in the Afar Region.

9.2.3. Solid Waste Handling

  • Recycle PP/HDPE packaging materials using an in-house recycling system.
  • Store and label spent ion-exchange resins safely and dispose through licensed hazardous waste contractors.
  • Implement a Waste Management Plan (WMP) covering segregation, storage, transport, and disposal procedures.
  • Conduct periodic waste audits and document results under the ISO 14001 environmental management system.

9.2.4. Noise Reduction

  • Enclose high-noise machinery in acoustic housings and install vibration dampeners.
  • Maintain green buffer zones and restrict access to high-noise areas.
  • Provide ear protection equipment and conduct routine occupational noise monitoring.
