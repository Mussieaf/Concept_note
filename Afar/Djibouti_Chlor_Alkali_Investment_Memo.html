Investment memorandum

750 TPD Caustic Soda
Project in Djibouti

A phased, export-led chlor-alkali complex at Damerjog, fed by Lake Assal salt and integrated with downstream chlorine derivatives for the Horn of Africa market.

Sustainable industry Export-led Membrane electrolysis Djibouti
01

Executive summary

The recommended project is a membrane-cell chlor-alkali plant with 750 TPD NaOH capacity, equivalent to about 270,000 tpa. Damerjog is the best location because it is closer to the Port of Djibouti, benefits from industrial-zone infrastructure, and is better suited to large-scale chemical handling than a remote lake site.

Lake Assal remains the logical salt source, but not necessarily the best place to house the main electrolysis plant, because the salt likely needs purification before chlor-alkali use. Djibouti's earlier project documents already show policy support for caustic soda and chlorine development around the Lake Assal—Ghoubet corridor.

02

Project basis

The plant would take salt or purified brine from Lake Assal/Ghoubet, purify it, run membrane electrolysis, and produce caustic soda, chlorine, and hydrogen. The project is export-led because Djibouti's domestic market is too small for a plant of this size.

A strong commercial setup would pair the chemical plant with downstream chlorine users, such as bleach, hydrochloric acid, or other industrial derivatives, to avoid value destruction from unmanaged chlorine. That integrated approach is the main reason the project can clear a bankability threshold.

Chlor-alkali sector activity in Africa — indicative of the industrial scale envisioned at Damerjog
03

Location rationale

Damerjog is the most competitive processing site if Lake Assal is treated as less feasible for the main plant. The site is described as a premium industrial zone near the Port of Djibouti, with preinstalled utilities and industrial planning aligned with large projects.

Ghoubet is still important as the raw-material and logistics node because the port is linked to the Lake Assal salt project. This suggests a two-node model: upstream salt extraction and shipment, downstream chemical conversion at Damerjog.

05

Capex & Opex

A realistic pre-FEED capex range is USD 550 million to USD 850 million for the core plant and standard utilities. If the project includes captive power, major desalination, chlorine derivatives, or large offsites, the total can approach USD 900 million to USD 1.2 billion.

A practical base case for an investment memo is USD 700 million. That assumes industrial-zone infrastructure is partly leveraged and does not require a fully self-contained utility island.

A competitive operating-cost range is USD 180 to USD 280 per tonne of caustic soda, excluding shipping. Electricity is the largest driver, followed by salt handling, purification chemicals, labor, maintenance, and logistics. A base-case opex assumption of about USD 220/t is reasonable for a first-pass model.

06

10-year P&L assumptions

Use the following base-case assumptions for a bankable-style model:

  • Capacity: 270,000 tpa
  • Utilization: 85% in Year 1, rising to 92% from Year 4 onward
  • NaOH price: USD 380/t in Year 1, rising gradually to USD 470/t by Year 10
  • Chlorine credit: USD 12 million in Year 1, rising to USD 21 million by Year 10
  • Hydrogen credit: USD 1.8 million per year
  • Discount rate: 14%
  • Tax rate: 20%
  • Depreciation: straight-line over 10 years
  • Base capex: USD 700 million

Financial view

The project should be modeled with three cases:

  • Conservative: high power cost, weak chlorine value, slower ramp-up
  • Base case: industrial-zone processing at Damerjog, moderate export pricing, partial chlorine monetization
  • Upside: long-term offtake contracts, integrated chlorine derivatives, and favorable utility economics

On a first-pass basis, the project is most likely to be financeable only if it secures:

  • Cheap and reliable power
  • Long-term export offtake
  • A chlorine monetization plan
  • A credible brine purification scheme
  • A strong EIA and safety framework
09

Strategic assessment

Strengths
  • Strategic port access and industrial-zone infrastructure
  • Lake Assal offers large salt availability
  • Government interest in salt-to-chemicals value addition
Weaknesses
  • High electricity intensity and utility dependence
  • Raw salt requires purification for chlor-alkali use
  • Domestic demand is far too small for the scale
Opportunities
  • Export to Ethiopia and the wider Horn of Africa
  • Downstream chlorine derivatives and hydrogen use
  • Two-node logistics model with Assal/Ghoubet and Damerjog
Threats
  • Power shortages or tariff escalation
  • Environmental and chlorine-safety permitting risk
  • Freight and port bottlenecks

Investment recommendation

🌿 Phased, export-led chlor-alkali at Damerjog
The best investment structure is a phased, export-led chlor-alkali project in Damerjog, fed by Lake Assal salt logistics and backed by a chlorine-derivatives strategy. That is the most competitive option if Lake Assal itself is considered less feasible for the main plant. The project is viable but economics depend heavily on power cost, chlorine monetization, and strong export offtake.

  • ✓ Lock in long-term export offtake
  • ✓ Build a chlorine monetization plan
  • ✓ Validate brine purification scheme
