How Money Is Actually Created

Most people operate under a fundamental misunderstanding about what money is and how it is created. Every part of the common understanding is incorrect.

The Great Misconception

Money is not a fixed thing. It does not exist in limited quantities. Banks do not lend out money that has been deposited by someone else. Governments can spend beyond taxes. And to get a loan, you do not need to prove you already have assets worth at least as much as the loan.

The economies that understand this fact have used it to build highways, railways, housing programmes, and technology industries. The economies that cling to the misunderstanding are still waiting for donor aid.

Kansas City Municipal Bond

Kansas City needed a new sewer system with no spare cash. It issued a Municipal Revenue Bond — a promise to repay over 30 years, backed by future sewer fees. Investors bought the bond today. The city received USD 800 million today. The money did not exist before the bond was issued. It was created by the promise of future revenue.

US Federal Reserve QE

After the 2008 crisis, the Fed purchased USD 3.7 trillion in securities. It did not have this cash in a vault. It created the money digitally — typing new numbers into accounts — to purchase assets representing future mortgage repayments and government tax revenues.

China's Infrastructure

China built more kilometres of high-speed railway between 2008 and 2020 than the rest of the world combined. It did not pay from accumulated savings. The China Development Bank issued Revenue Rights Asset-Backed Securities — promises backed by future toll revenues.

The Bank of England Proof

Professor Richard Werner conducted landmark research with the Bank of England itself. Their official conclusion: "Banks do not lend out money that has been deposited with them. When a bank makes a loan, it creates a deposit in the borrower's account, effectively creating new money."

The SBLC Demystified

A Standby Letter of Credit looks similar to an LC on paper but serves a fundamentally different purpose. The word "Standby" is the key. It stands by. It waits. It is a contingent commitment — a formal assurance that if a defined trigger condition occurs, the bank will perform.

Credit Enhancement, Not Payment

In the MAEC framework, the SBLC issued by a local bank is a credit enhancement instrument — not a payment guarantee. Its purpose is to improve the creditworthiness of the project sufficiently that MAEC's international banking platform can confidently channel capital.

It is the difference between a project being "unrated and uninvestable" and being "credit-enhanced and bankable."

Why "Cash Backed" Does Not Mean Cash Blocked

"Cash backed" means the SBLC is secured by near-cash assets — assets with defined, contractually certain future cash value: future PPA revenues, future toll revenues, future mineral royalty streams, or PRBS certificates representing hypothecated future income.

Why the Bank Does Not Need to Block Cash

Four reasons: (1) Basel III CCF Structure — 50% Credit Conversion Factor, not 100%. (2) Off-balance-sheet treatment under IAS 37/IFRS 7. (3) PRBS creates a positive balance sheet asset. (4) MAEC executes a legally binding Indemnity Agreement confirming no claim will ever be made.

Single Obligor Limit — A Non-Issue

Because SBLCs are off-balance-sheet contingent instruments, not direct loans, they do not count toward the Single Obligor Limit in most regulatory frameworks. A bank can issue an SBLC for a project many multiples of what it could directly lend.

The Rescue Helicopter Metaphor

An LC is like a taxi — you pay when you arrive. An SBLC is like a rescue helicopter on standby. You hope you never need it. You almost certainly will not. But its mere presence gives confidence to everyone that the operation can proceed safely.

The Indemnity Agreement

The Indemnity Agreement is MAEC's answer to the residual concern: what if something goes wrong? It is a legally binding document that transfers risk away from the issuing bank and onto MAEC.

— The Bank's Shield

What the Indemnity Does

Indemnifies the bank from any negative occurrence to the SBLC MT760 instrument — any damage, loss of value, civil or criminal suit. Explicitly confirms the SBLC is issued purely for credit enhancement and MAEC will never make a drawing claim. Protects from third-party claims.

The Temporal Firewall

The SBLC has a validity of 1 year and 1 day. The MAEC loan's grace period is 3 to 5 years. The project repayment obligations begin only after the grace period. The SBLC expires before any repayment obligation ever falls due. It is structurally impossible to claim against it.

Hypothecation, the PRBS & Making Future Revenue Bankable

The Oldest Trick in Finance

Hypothecation comes from the Greek hypotheke, meaning "pledge." Under a hypothec, a debtor pledges an asset as security — but crucially, retains possession and use of the asset while the pledge is in force.

Your mortgage is a hypothecation. You pledge your house but continue to live in it. MAEC applies this ancient principle to modern assets: future project revenue.

The PRBS Certificate

The Project Revenue-Backed Security converts hypothecated future revenue into a bankable, investable asset. Think of it as a certificate of ownership over the future revenue stream — similar to how a land title is a certificate of ownership over physical land.

How PRBS Works

Future revenues are formally assigned to the SPV via a Revenue Assignment Deed. The SPV issues a PRBS Certificate. The local bank holds the PRBS as a Held-to-Maturity Debt Security — an asset on its balance sheet. The bank issues the SBLC backed by the PRBS.

What is SIMPA?

Sovereign Infrastructure Monetization and Project Acceleration — MAEC's complete structured finance architecture for sovereign infrastructure projects. It encompasses revenue identification, SPV constitution, revenue assignment, SBLC credit enhancement, and loan deployment.

Why Does MAEC Need Your SBLC?

Four Fundamental Reasons

1. Regulatory Compliance: Local banks are the regulated gateway. MAEC cannot legally deploy capital directly without routing through the regulated banking system.

2. Risk Transfer: The SBLC transforms the risk profile from "uncollateralised African project" to "loan secured by SBLC from regulated local bank."

3. Leverage: One SBLC unlocks multiple times its face value through credit enhancement, insurance, and structured engineering.

4. Proof of Local Commitment: The local bank's SBLC demonstrates institutional commitment — skin in the game.

This is not too good to be true. Every element has been used in traceable, verifiable, public transactions. The Ilute Solar Project in Zambia, the Chisamba 100MW plant, Nigeria's FGN Sukuk, Kenya's Infrastructure Bond Programme — all validate these components.
