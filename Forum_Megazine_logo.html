FORUM OVERVIEW

The African First Ladies Initiative is the premier continental platform engaged in advocacy, resource mobilization, and producing women leaders in climate action contributing to Africa's sustainable development. Its mission aligns with SDG 1 (No Poverty), SDG 7 (Affordable Clean Energy), SDG 9 (Industry & Innovation), and SDG 13 (Climate Action).

Community relationships are essential for strengthening and developing high-quality climate services and sharing resources for mutual benefit. Organizing forums, exhibitions, and training programs are strategies that assist in establishing a network among stakeholders including our strategic partners at Stockholm Environment Institute and Africa International Policy Research Center.

International forums are a significant source of visibility for climate action, and people who attend become aware of the Initiative's purpose of empowering women, applying and generating green knowledge, and connecting our communities.

OBJECTIVE OF THE FORUM

The Forum assists women leaders and entrepreneurs with all aspects of climate adaptation, including choosing green technologies, exploring sustainable livelihoods, developing relevant skills, and accessing climate finance.

THE CLIMATE
CHALLENGE
WE MUST FACE

Have you ever considered the true cost of inaction on climate change? The writer Wangari Maathai became a Nobel laureate because of her Green Belt Movement and advocacy for sustainable development after witnessing environmental degradation in Kenya. She reminds us that the biggest mistake we could make is focusing on short-term economic gains over long-term planetary health.

Carbon flow in our ecosystem focuses on four principal ideas: emissions, sequestration, adaptation, and mitigation. Emissions are activities that release greenhouse gases, whereas sequestration involves capturing and storing carbon. These dynamics are such that a single activity could be either a source or a sink depending on how we manage it.

I can't think of anything better than clean cooking initiatives to further explain this idea. A cookstove can be an asset if it reduces deforestation and improves health. It could also be an asset if it creates carbon credits that fund further climate action, supported by partners like TOYO Solar and Globo Hydro Power.

Africa, as a continent striving for sustainable development, needs women at the forefront of climate action. The question now is not whether women should lead; it is how we ensure every woman has the tools to do so, with financial backing from institutions like Bank of Abyssinia.

Emission Reduction & Technological Leap

Another good topic to explain climate finance is debt. Most people will say debt is a burden. But is it really? It depends. If debt results in a climate-resilient infrastructure with net positive impact, it obviously is an asset. Green bonds in Kenya have funded renewable energy projects that pay dividends for decades.

"Constraints are inevitable, yes. But rather than accepting them, we can discover and pay attention to them. We can recognize their value. When we notice constraints but don't let them define our possibilities, we can flip them to create opportunity."
- Dr. Laura Huang, Harvard Business School

The youth is encouraged by the educational system to be either an employee or specialist. The problem is that as long as you remain a specialist without systems thinking, you will miss the big picture. Here comes the answer to the challenge we face: students should be taught to be systems entrepreneurs who invest in climate solutions.

This is where the African First Ladies Initiative comes in. This platform plays an important role in helping women reimagine their role in climate action by providing training on green entrepreneurship and clean technology, supported by our partners at Union Global Investment.

WHAT IS CLEAN COOKING?

Clean cooking refers to the use of energy-efficient stoves and fuels that minimize emissions, reduce deforestation, and improve health outcomes. Defined more precisely, it is a development strategy to fast-track the transition from traditional biomass to modern energy solutions, certified by Ethiopian Conformity Assessment Enterprises.

🏠 For Households

Reduce indoor air pollution, save time on fuel collection, improve family health.

🌍 For Climate

Lower black carbon emissions, reduce deforestation pressure, generate carbon credits.

CLEAN COOKING TECHNOLOGY

Women spend an average of 4 hours daily collecting firewood. Clean cooking gives them that time back for education, enterprise, and rest. TOYO Solar provides the energy solutions that make this transition possible.

Year of Climate Action

2026 is an initiative of the African Union along with international partners that is intended to encourage African nations to accelerate climate adaptation and emission reduction. It was formally launched at the African Climate Summit as a program for nations to unite behind common goals.

The year 2026 is symbolic as it commemorates the midpoint to the 2030 Sustainable Development Goals. The program recognizes women's achievements in climate action.
