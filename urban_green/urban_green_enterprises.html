01

Food Waste & Yard Waste

The cycle begins where most see an ending. Organic scraps from households, markets, and restaurants—along with fallen leaves and garden trimmings—are collected as raw material rather than discarded. In Addis Ababa, this represents tonnes of daily waste diverted from landfills and transformed into productive inputs.

100% Organic Diversion
Zero Landfill Waste
02

Collection & Transportation

Youth-led collection teams gather organic waste from designated points across each sub-city. Specialized transport ensures materials reach composting sites fresh and uncontaminated. This stage creates immediate green jobs while establishing the logistical backbone of the circular system.

11 Sub-City Networks
Daily Collection Routes
03

Composting & Processing

Collected organic matter undergoes controlled decomposition. Youth enterprises manage aerated compost piles, monitoring temperature and moisture to produce nutrient-rich soil amendments. This biological factory operates 24/7, converting waste into black gold within 8–12 weeks.

8–12 Weeks Processing
40% Cost Reduction
04

Compost Distribution

Finished compost is packaged and distributed to urban farms, tree nurseries, and restoration sites. Youth enterprises manage inventory, quality testing, and delivery logistics. This distribution network ensures every gram of processed waste returns to the soil where it belongs.

100% Closed Loop
Local Distribution
05

Soil Amendments

Compost enriches depleted urban soils, restoring fertility and structure without chemical fertilizers. Youth teams apply amendments to rooftop gardens, vertical farms, and nursery beds. The soil microbiome awakens—bacteria, fungi, and earthworms create living ground capable of sustaining intensive urban agriculture.

Zero Chemical Inputs
3x Soil Fertility
06

Plant Life

Seeds become seedlings, seedlings become crops. Vegetables, herbs, and medicinal plants flourish in amended soils across vertical gardens, rooftop farms, and container systems. Bees pollinate flowers, increasing yields by 30%. Tree nurseries produce seedlings for city greening contracts. Life returns to concrete.

$8K–12K Crop Revenue
30% Yield Increase
07

Distribution & Consumption

Fresh produce travels meters—not miles—to local markets, restaurants, and households. Honey, herbal oils, and seedlings reach buyers through pre-arranged agreements. Revenue flows back to youth enterprises, funding wages, reinvestment, and the next growing cycle. The loop closes economically.

$20K–30K Total Revenue
Local Supply Chain
08

Food Donation & Community

Surplus harvests feed vulnerable communities through donation networks. Youth associations allocate a percentage of production to food security programs, strengthening social bonds. What cannot be sold or donated returns to the compost pile—completing the infinite cycle. Nothing is wasted. Everything transforms.

5K–7K Indirect Beneficiaries

The Architecture of Self-Sufficiency

We design for the exit from day one. Donor funding covers setup, training, and initial operations. By month eighteen, each enterprise stands on its own feet—diversified, market-driven, and youth-owned.

When honey prices dip, herbal oils sustain. When vegetable demand wavers, seedling contracts pay. This is not a safety net—it is a safety web, woven from multiple revenue strands.

"The design assumes no perpetual subsidy. Recurring costs are covered by enterprise revenue after year two."
