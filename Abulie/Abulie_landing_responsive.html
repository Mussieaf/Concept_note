The Catalogue

A magazine of stone & ore.

Hover any plate to read its story. Every specimen ships with a matching footnote description.

◆ Crushed Stone & Aggregate — hard-rock gravel screened into controlled grades; the essential skeleton of concrete, roads and rail ballast, supplied by the tonne.
◆ Kyanite — Al₂SiO₅. Sky-blue bladed crystals with a curious split personality: hardness 4.5 along the length, 7 across it. A premium refractory and ceramic raw material.
◆ Emerald (Green Beryl) — Be₃Al₂Si₆O₁₈ colored green by chromium/vanadium. Treasured for millennia; the May birthstone and one of the "big four" gems.
◆ Gold Doré — a semi-pure alloy of gold and silver cast at the mine, typically 85–95% Au, refined to bullion purity by the buyer. The raw heartbeat of the precious-metals trade.
◆ Pyrite — FeS₂. Brass-yellow metallic luster, pale green-black streak; cubic crystal habit. Sulfuric-acid feedstock and a favorite of mineral collectors.
◆ Labradorite — a plagioclase feldspar famed for labradorescence — flashes of blue, green and gold that appear as the stone turns. Prized for cabochons and slabs.
◆ Lepidolite — K(Li,Al)₃(Si,Al)₄O₁₀(F,OH)₂. A lilac to rose-pink mica and one of the richest sources of lithium — the metal powering the world's batteries.
◆ Smoky Quartz — SiO₂ colored brown by natural irradiation. Transparent, hexagonal prisms; a durable (Mohs 7) favorite for jewelry and carving.
◆ Graphite — crystalline carbon: steel-gray to black, greasy feel, marks paper. Essential for batteries, electrodes, lubricants and — famously — pencils.
◆ Chromite — FeCr₂O₄, the only commercial chromium ore. Black-brown, submetallic luster, very dense; the backbone of stainless steel and ferrochrome.
◆ Milky Quartz — common SiO₂ made opaque by countless fluid inclusions. Sacred in many cultures; today a workhorse of glass, ceramics and electronics.
◆ Lithium Pegmatite — an exceptionally coarse igneous rock where leftover magma grows meter-scale crystals of quartz, feldspar and lithium micas — a direct source battery-metal feedstock.
◆ Pegmatite — the coarsest rock on Earth, with interlocking crystals of quartz, feldspar and mica several centimeters across. Mother-rock of many gems and rare elements.
◆ Mica Schist — a metamorphic rock layered with sparkling muscovite mica. Splits along its foliation; valued for decorative walling, paving and natural finishes.
◆ Tourmaline Pegmatite — feldspar–quartz matrix streaked with black schorl tourmaline (NaFe₃Al₆(BO₃)₃Si₆O₁₈(OH)₄) — a classic combination from lithium-rich pegmatite fields.
◆ Magnetite — Fe₃O₄, the most magnetic mineral on Earth. Black, submetallic, ~72% iron — the highest-grade iron ore known and vital to the steel industry.
◆ Conglomerate — sedimentary rock of rounded pebbles bound in a sandy matrix — natural "pudding stone". A characterful choice for feature walls and landscaping.
◆ Kyanite (weathered) — blade fragments with rust-brown iron-oxide staining at the surface; the blue interior remains the same prized aluminosilicate used in high-alumina refractories.
◆ Massive Milky Quartz — frosted white lumps of silicon dioxide, Mohs hardness 7. Raw material for glass, foundry sands, and lapidary work worldwide.
◆ Muscovite Mica Schist — silvery to rose-tinted mica sheets within a schistose matrix; muscovite (KAl₂(AlSi₃O₁₀)(OH)₂) is valued for insulation, paints and pearlescent finishes.
