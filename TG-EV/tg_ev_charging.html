Powering Ethiopia's Green Future

Where Charging Meets Care

Ethiopia's first integrated urban mobility hub in Kazanchis, Addis Ababa — combining 120kW DC fast charging with premium automatic car wash, battery energy storage, and sustainable water recycling.

Explore the Vision View Investment

Ethiopia has become Africa's most electrified vehicle market. With the world's first ICE import ban and surging EV adoption, the time to build is now.

Policy Tailwinds

World's first complete ICE import ban (Jan 2024). Duty-free EV equipment imports through 2026. "Green Lane" tax breaks for investors.

Explosive Growth

EV fleet surged from 7,000 (2023) to ~115,000 (2026). 60%+ of new registrations are electric. Only ~500 public chargers nationally.

Dwell-Time Monetization

20 minutes of charging becomes 20 minutes of productive service. Car wash, detailing, lounge, and retail — all while you charge.

99%+ Uptime Guarantee

400kWh BESS + 60kW solar canopy ensures uninterrupted service despite grid instability. Operational excellence as brand promise.

Kazanchis: The Heart of Transformation

Kazanchis is undergoing a government-led corridor development transformation — from a degraded neighborhood to a modern downtown commercial hub with improved multi-lane boulevards, new parking, and enhanced commercial infrastructure.

This creates a premium, high-traffic location ideal for a mobility hub. The project is positioned at the intersection of two high-growth sectors, capitalizing on Ethiopia's aggressive E-Mobility Strategy.

Strategic market assessment for the integrated EV charging and car wash hub in Addis Ababa.

1

Threat of New Entrants

Medium-High

Policy support and import incentives attract entrants, but land, power access, and ~100M ETB capital create significant barriers. First-mover advantage in premium sites is critical.

2

Supplier Power

High

Chargers, wash systems, BESS, transformers and spare parts are imported; FX-sensitive. ~25% of generation capacity inactive due to spare parts shortages. Diversified sourcing essential.

3

Buyer Power

Medium

Individual consumers are price aware, but fleet buyers can exert strong pressure. Fleet contracts with annual lock-ins reduce buyer power and stabilize utilization.

4

Threat of Substitutes

Medium

Home/workplace charging can replace some public charging; informal washing substitutes for basic wash demand. Integrated experience is the moat against substitution.

5

Competitive Rivalry

Rising

Still early locally, but likely to intensify quickly as state-linked and fuel-retail players expand. Differentiation through integrated experience, not price, is the winning strategy.

Benchmarking against local and global competitors to define our unique positioning.

Ethio Telecom

Local Strength: Brand Trust

National infrastructure enabler with government alignment and capital access. Three hubs along Bole Road served 165,000+ EVs in 10 months. Weakness: Undifferentiated experience, slower private-sector agility.

Fuel Stations (Total, Shell, Oilibya)

Local Strength: Existing Network

Hybrid forecourt model with existing land, traffic, parking, and known customer behavior. Convenient legacy network. Weakness: Legacy layouts poor for long-dwell charging; slow adaptation to EV needs.

Independent Local Car Washes

Local Weakness: Low Standardization

Transactional wash operators with local familiarity and flexible pricing. Low-price competition. Weakness: Low standardization, limited technology, weak premium experience — ripe for disruption.

ChargePoint

Global Strength: Network Platform

Smart network leader with hardware + software monetization. Large ecosystem and fleet orientation. 342,000+ charging spots globally. Not yet in Ethiopia. Requires network density to succeed.

Tesla Supercharger

Global Strength: Gold Standard UX

Premium but value-per-minute strong. Integrated vehicle + charge ecosystem. 60,000+ Superchargers worldwide. Closed ecosystem requires captive demand. Sets the bar for charging experience globally.

Understanding our position to build defensibility beyond hardware ownership.

🌿 Strengths

  • Integrated charging + wash concept creates cross-sell potential
  • Kazanchis strategically strong due to traffic and urban redevelopment
  • Policy tailwinds and equipment incentives improve entry economics
  • BESS + solar = uptime differentiation in grid-unstable environment
  • 90%+ water recycling and professional wash operations

⚡ Weaknesses

  • Heavy upfront CAPEX and imported equipment dependency
  • Base-case financial assumptions may be aggressive
  • Public charging can become commoditized without differentiation
  • Grid dependence threatens service reliability without backup

🚀 Opportunities

  • Fleet partnerships with ride-hailing, delivery, and corporate operators
  • Memberships, subscriptions, and bundled services
  • Retail monetization of dwell time (lounge, coffee, convenience)
  • Expansion into a replicable network format across Addis Ababa

⚠️ Threats

  • Rapid entry by telecom, fuel stations, or state-backed players
  • FX shocks, import delays, spare-part shortages
  • Tariff regulation or public pressure on charging prices
  • Lower-than-expected EV traffic in target micro-location
