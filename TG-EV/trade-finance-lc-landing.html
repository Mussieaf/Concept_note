01 — The Transaction

A structure engineered
for both sides of the sea.

This arrangement pairs a Sight LC with an Irrevocable Usance LC so the supplier ships with total payment security while the importer keeps cash working at home.

Under the commercial contract, the importer commits only a fraction of the contract value before goods ever leave the factory. The balance travels under bank obligation, not trust — governed by UCP standards and verified through the international banking channel.

The issuing bank in Ethiopia opens the credit; an A-rated confirming bank adds its own undertaking, neutralizing country and counterparty risk. Third-party documents are acceptable, partial shipments are allowed, and the credit is transferable only through the advising corridor.

“The balance travels under bank obligation, not trust.”

Incoterms are structured CIF Djibouti or FOB China, with the LC expiring in the beneficiary’s country so documents never chase deadlines across borders.

03 — The Process

From signature to settlement,
in nine movements.

A documentary credit lives on paperwork discipline. Each step below is executed through the banking channel — paper first, cargo second, payment last.

Movement 01

Contract & Application

Importer and supplier sign the commercial contract (10% Sight + 90% Usance). The importer applies to the issuing bank under agreed terms.

Movement 02

Transmission & Confirmation

The LC travels through the A-rated confirming / advising bank to the supplier’s bank in China. Production begins on receipt.

Movement 03

Production & Shipment

Goods are prepared, shipped and cleared for export. Shipping and customs documents are assembled to LC specification.

Movement 04

Presentation

The supplier’s bank forwards documents through the banking channel to the issuing bank for examination.

Movement 05

10% Released at Sight

On full document compliance, the sight portion is paid immediately — the supplier’s guaranteed partial liquidity.

Movement 06

90% Usance Acceptance

The balance is formally accepted and scheduled at 180/360-day maturity. The importer clears goods in Ethiopia.

Movement 07

Revenue Cycle

Imported goods are sold into the local market; proceeds build the settlement pool alongside operating cash flow.

Movement 08

Maturity

At maturity the issuing bank pays the deferred 90% to the supplier’s bank, completing the transaction.

Movement 09

Facility Renewal

A clean repayment record positions the importer for the next cycle — larger lines, longer tenors.

05 — Risk Architecture

Where each risk goes to die.

The structure pushes every major exposure onto the party best positioned to carry it.

For the Bank

Irrevocable obligation

An irrevocable LC locks the payment duty. A-rated confirmation strips out country and counterparty risk, while the 10% sight tranche lowers the financed exposure from day one.

For the Supplier

Guaranteed, confirmed payment

A confirmed LC converts buyer risk into bank risk — with 10% received immediately upon compliant presentation, well before maturity of the balance.

For the Importer

Liquidity preserved

Only a tenth of the contract value leaves the treasury up front. The 360-day runway lets revenue from the goods themselves fund the settlement.
